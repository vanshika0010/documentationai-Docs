<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I'm new to international shipping — what should I prepare before sending an order abroad?">

You'll typically need a commercial invoice with product details, value, and buyer info, HS codes for customs classification, a valid KYC from the buyer in some countries, and properly packed products with clear labels. You should also check if your product is allowed in the destination country.

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is a commercial invoice, and why is it required for international shipments?">

A commercial invoice is like your product's passport. It includes product description, quantity, price, seller and buyer details, and the HS code. Customs officials use it to calculate duties and decide whether your shipment can be cleared.

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Who pays the customs duties and taxes — the buyer or the seller?">

That depends on how you set up your shipping terms. With DDU (Delivered Duty Unpaid), the buyer pays the duties at delivery. With DDP (Delivered Duty Paid), you cover the taxes upfront.

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What are HS codes, and how do they affect my international shipments?">

HS (Harmonized System) codes classify products for customs. These codes determine how much duty is charged and whether the item is allowed into a country. You can find the correct HS code based on your product type.

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
